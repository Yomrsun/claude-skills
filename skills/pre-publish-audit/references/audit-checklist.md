# Audit Checklist

Run in this order. Traps 1-6 are the ones that bite most often.

## 1. Unvalidated claims
Every factual claim about the product or results must be in the VALIDATED section of the claim ledger: specs, dimensions, weight, speed, compatibility, ingredients, materials, stock, price, ratings, "best", "first", "only", "#1", customer counts, results.
- "It's visible in our photo" is not validation.
- "Everyone knows this" is not validation.
- A PENDING claim is a BLOCK, however true it feels.

## 2. Measurement claims disguised as description
Words that imply a measurement or comparison you can't cite: "fits in any bag", "lasts all day", "half the size", "lightning fast", "whisper quiet", "fits where others don't".
Test: does this imply a number? If yes and the number isn't validated, rewrite around the mechanism or design, not the measurement. ("Rolls up and clips shut" instead of "half the size.")

## 3. Banned phrases and their paraphrases
Check the REFUSED list and brand laws. Then look for the same idea in new words. A retired claim restated more literally is still the retired claim. If the brand has banned "feels like new", then "good as new" and "you'll never know it's used" are the same violation.

## 4. The hidden "coming soon"
State the product's real stage: concept → prototype → validated → pre-order → shipping → supported.
- "Coming soon" with no stage is a FIX.
- A date with no stage attached ("Arrives March 3") is "coming soon" with a calendar on it. Name the stage: "Pre-orders open March 3, shipping in April."
- Never imply a later stage than the true one.

## 5. Implied competitor comparisons
- Named comparisons need: a same-tier product (not a cheaper or pro-tier model), a cited source, and owner approval recorded in the ledger.
- Unnamed digs still argue with a competitor: "not another flimsy knockoff", "unlike the big brands". Rewrite positively: say what this product is.
- Comparing your base model with a rival's premium model, or vice versa, is a BLOCK.

## 6. The comment-section test
Read every subject line, headline, hook and on-screen line as the most sarcastic commenter would.
- Does it invite an easy joke at the brand's expense? ("Our biggest drop yet" on a phone case → "so it breaks?")
- Does it make a promise the reader can call out? ("Talk to a real human, anytime" → "then why did nobody answer my email?")
- Subject lines and on-screen text matter most: they travel without the caption.

## 7. Tagline and lock-up rules
If the brand laws define a required line and where it goes (short form beside the logo, long form where the logo is absent), check every piece carries the right one in the right place. Missing entirely is a FIX.

## 8. Promises the business can't keep
- Opt-in promises ("one email when it launches") must match the actual send plan.
- Shipping times, guarantees, return windows and support hours must match the brain word for word.
- "Free" must have no hidden condition, or the condition must be stated next to it.

## 9. Pricing and urgency truthfulness
- "Was" or compare-at prices must be prices the item was genuinely offered at, openly and recently, for a real period of time. A price set briefly just to make a discount look bigger fails. Local rules on reference pricing vary; flag doubtful cases.
- Countdown timers and "only 3 left" must be true.
- No fake deadlines; if the sale ends, it ends when stated.

## 10. Endorsements and testimonials
- Testimonials and reviews must be real, attributable and permissioned; don't edit them so they say something new. Invented, bought or AI-written reviews are a BLOCK (in the US, the FTC's consumer-review rule bans them).
- Paid, gifted or affiliate relationships must be disclosed clearly and up front (#ad, "Paid partnership", "I got this free"), following the endorsement rules in the market you sell to.
- No implied endorsement by people, brands or organizations that haven't agreed.

## 11. Regulated territory
Health, safety, financial, environmental ("eco", "green", "sustainable", "carbon neutral"), children's products, and age-restricted goods carry extra rules. Flag any such claim as BLOCK unless the brain's brand laws and the ledger explicitly cover it, and recommend the owner check with the relevant authority or advisor.

## 12. Platform rules
Ads and posts must fit the platform's ad policies (restricted categories, before/after images, personal-attribute targeting language like "Are you struggling with…?"). Flag anything likely to be rejected.

## 13. Voice and brand
Compare against the voice section: tone words, banned words, reading level. Voice drift is a FIX, not a BLOCK, unless brand laws make it a hard rule.

## 14. Accessibility and basics
- Alt text on images, captions on video, readable contrast on text over images.
- Links go where they say; UTM tags present if the data-analyst asked for them.
- Marketing emails carry a working unsubscribe and the sender's postal address; marketing texts name the business and say how to opt out.
- Names, prices, dates and product names spelled exactly as in the brain.

## 15. Internal consistency
- If one claim is blocked, its twin is blocked everywhere in the campaign.
- The email, the landing page and the ad must say the same price, date and offer.
